Audio

                    
                    

                        Every other check judges a title, and a title can be wrong about what the file holds.
                        This one measures the audio, so a recap, a reaction or a stretch of dialogue is thrown
                        away before it reaches your library. Files under a minute are passed without an
                        opinion — the measure cannot tell music from speech at that length.
                    

                    

                        
                        

                            Measured on a 66-clip corpus, music fell between 0.31 and 5.57 and speech between
                            6.20 and 12.32. Anything between the two values goes to the review queue rather
                            than being decided either way. The measured value is recorded against every item,
                            so you can see where your own library actually sits.
                        

                    

                    

                        
                    


                    

                        
                    

                    
                    

                        Jellyfin plays themes at the same volume as everything else and offers no theme volume
                        control, so without this a theme is as loud as whoever uploaded it made it. Normalising
                        at encode time fixes that on every client.
                    

                    

                        
                        
-23 is the broadcast standard. Use a higher value such as -18 if themes feel too quiet.

                    

                    

                        
                    

                    

                        
                    

                    

                        
                    

                    

                        
                    


                    
Catalogues

                    

                        A catalogue is asked before any searching, using the item's own TMDB or TheTVDB id.
                        It either has the theme for that exact title or it does not — there is nothing to
                        score and nothing to get wrong — so a hit is used as it stands.
                    

                    
                    

                        A community-curated index that maps a work's TMDB id to the video someone chose as its
                        theme — roughly 4400 films, 1300 shows and 140 film collections. Asked first for
                        everything. A film with no theme of its own inherits its collection's, so Alien³
                        gets the Alien Collection theme. It supplies a link, so the download is unchanged.
                    

                    
                    

                        ThemerrDB publishes once a day at 12:00 UTC, and the scheduled task reads it an hour
                        later. Without the local copy, every title in your library costs one request just to
                        find out it is not in the database — and most are not. With it, a miss costs
                        nothing. Turning this off does not disable ThemerrDB; it goes back to asking about
                        each item directly.
                    

                    

                        
                        

                            The safety net for a server that was switched off when the task was due.
                        

                    

                    
                    

                        Off by default, and worth a moment's thought before turning on. Coverage is good
                        — roughly 43% of American series — and a hit is certain in a way no search
                        result is. But these files are hosted by Plex for Plex's own clients, and the fair-use
                        rationale Plex publishes for them covers their software, not this plugin. Whether that
                        is acceptable on your server is your decision, not ours to make for you.

Removing themes

                    

                        Deletes every theme.mp3 ThemeForge wrote into your library. Files you have
                        replaced by hand are left alone — each one is checked against the contents
                        ThemeForge recorded when it wrote it, and anything that no longer matches is kept.
                        


                        Uninstalling ThemeForge does not do this. It removes the plugin's own data and
                        leaves your theme files where they are.
                    

                    

                    
Starting over

                    

                        Throws away every decision ThemeForge has recorded, so your library is looked at again
                        from nothing. Worth doing after an upgrade that changes how matches are chosen: a
                        recorded decision is only as good as the code that made it, and several of the states
                        they are kept in — rejected, locked — are treated as your judgement and
                        would never be revisited on their own.
                        


                        Ticking the box also deletes the theme files ThemeForge wrote. Anything you have
                        replaced by hand since is kept either way.
