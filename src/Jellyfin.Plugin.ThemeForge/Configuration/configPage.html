Audio

                    
                    

                        Every other check judges a title, and a title can be wrong about what the file holds.
                        This one measures the audio, so a recap, a reaction or a stretch of dialogue is thrown
                        away before it reaches your library. Files under a minute are passed without an
                        opinion — the measure cannot tell music from speech at that length.
                    

                    

                        
                        

                            Measured on a 66-clip corpus, music fell between 0.31 and 5.57 and speech between
                            6.20 and 12.32. Anything between the two values goes to the review queue rather
                            than being decided either way. The measured value is recorded against every item,
                            so you can see where your own library actually sits.
                        

                    

                    

                        
                    


                    
Audio processing

                    

                        By default a theme is written exactly as it was downloaded: the same audio stream at the
                        same volume, kept in the format it arrived in (Opus, AAC or MP3). Everything below is off
                        unless you turn it on, and a file is only re-encoded when one of these has to change it.
                    

                    
                    

                        Brings a theme that is quieter than the floor below up to it. Never lowers anything: a
                        theme already at or above the floor is left exactly as it is.
                    

                    

                        
                        
-16 is roughly where streaming services level music. Most uploads are already above it.

                    

                    
                    

                        Every theme ends up at the target below, whether that means raising it or lowering it.
                        When this is on it takes precedence over raising quiet themes.
                    

                    

                        
                    

                    

                        
                    

                    
                    

                        
                    

                    

                        
                    

                    

                        
                    

                    
                    

                        Off, a theme keeps the codec it was delivered in and loses nothing; Jellyfin transcodes
                        for the rare client that cannot play it. Turn this on if you would rather every theme
                        were an MP3 to begin with.
                    

                    

                        
                    


                    
Themes already written

                    

                        Themes written before version 2.3 were normalised to -23 LUFS and faded, which made most
                        of them quieter than the upload they came from. This fetches every theme ThemeForge wrote
                        again, from the same source, and writes it with the settings above. Nothing else changes:
                        states, scores and your review decisions stay as they are, and a theme you have replaced
                        by hand is left alone. Save your settings first.
                    

                    

                    
Catalogues

                    

                        A catalogue is asked before any searching, using the item's own TMDB or TheTVDB id.
                        It either has the theme for that exact title or it does not — there is nothing to
                        score and nothing to get wrong — so a hit is used as it stands.
                    

                    
                    

                        A community-curated index that maps a work's TMDB id to the video someone chose as its
                        theme — roughly 4400 films, 1300 shows and 140 film collections. Asked first for
                        everything. A film with no theme of its own inherits its collection's, so Alien³
                        gets the Alien Collection theme. It supplies a link, so the download is unchanged.
                    

                    
                    

                        ThemerrDB publishes once a day at 12:00 UTC, and the scheduled task reads it an hour
                        later. Without the local copy, every title in your library costs one request just to
                        find out it is not in the database — and most are not. With it, a miss costs
                        nothing. Turning this off does not disable ThemerrDB; it goes back to asking about
                        each item directly.
                    

                    

                        
                        

                            The safety net for a server that was switched off when the task was due.
                        

                    

                    
                    

                        Off by default, and worth a moment's thought before turning on. Coverage is good
                        — roughly 43% of American series — and a hit is certain in a way no search
                        result is. But these files are hosted by Plex for Plex's own clients, and the fair-use
                        rationale Plex publishes for them covers their software, not this plugin. Whether that
                        is acceptable on your server is your decision, not ours to make for you.
                    


                    
Who wrote the music

                    

                        The composer's name is the most specific thing a search can ask for:
                        “Firefly Main Title — Greg Edmonson” cannot be about any other Firefly.
                        Jellyfin records a composer for very few items, and without one the ladder loses its
                        composer rung, an upload that names the composer earns no bonus, and a title that is an
                        ordinary word — Lost, Alien — is held below the auto-assign
                        score for want of anything to corroborate it.
                    

                    
                    

                        Asks Wikidata and MusicBrainz by the title's own IMDb or TMDB id, never by name.
                        Both are public-domain databases, and answers are kept for two months, so a whole
                        library costs a handful of requests once. Your library's own credits are never
                        replaced; this only fills in what is missing. When the theme is a song, its title
                        and performer are searched for first — “The Sopranos Woke Up This
                        Morning Alabama 3” — and an upload titled by song and artist is
                        recognised as the show's theme instead of being rejected for not naming the show.
                    

                    
                    

                        Runs at 02:00 UTC, an hour before the discovery run. Turning it off does not disable the
                        research; each run then looks up only what it happens to need, which spreads the same
                        requests through the run instead of getting them over with.
                    

                    
                    

                        Wikidata answers about three quarters of a library in a handful of requests. MusicBrainz
                        covers much of the rest — it is what finds the composers of Sherlock and Rick and
                        Morty — but at one request per second, so it is the slow half of the job. Only its
                        lookup by IMDb id is used: searching it by title returns the 1978 composer for the 2004
                        Battlestar Galactica.
                    

                    
                    

                        Wikidata records the theme of only the most famous shows. The article's infobox
                        records it for far more — The Sopranos, Firefly, House, Mad Men — and
                        often names who wrote the theme, which is not always who scored the show: Dexter's
                        theme is Rolfe Kent's, its score Daniel Licht's. Series only, five articles to a
                        request, reached through the Wikidata record rather than searched for by name.
                        Wikipedia does limit how fast it is asked; anything it turns away is asked about on
                        the next run.
                    

                    
                    

                        The last resort, and the only source read against the site's wishes: IMDb answers a
                        request that says it is a program with 403, and a browser-shaped one with a
                        bot check, and its terms do not permit automated reading. Asked only about titles
                        Wikidata and Wikipedia left without a theme, and only by IMDb id. An entry is taken only
                        when the listing calls it the title music or names it after the work — for a film
                        the listing is the licensed songs in the order they play, so its first entry is whatever
                        plays first rather than the theme. Use Test the IMDb lookup below to
                        find out whether your server can read it at all.
                    

                    
                    

                        IMDb's bot check clears only when a browser engine runs its script. A plain request is
                        tried first because it is far quicker and works where IMDb is not challenging the server.
                        The check is applied per address and is much harsher on hosted servers than on home
                        connections.
                    

                    
                    

                        A one-off download of roughly 150–200 MB into ThemeForge's tools folder beside
                        yt-dlp, which expands to about 400 MB. Covers Linux, macOS and Windows on both x64
                        and arm64. You can start it yourself, and undo it, below.
                    

                    
                    

                        Chrome's sandbox cannot start inside most container images, which is how Jellyfin is
                        usually run, so this is on. Turn it off if your server runs Jellyfin outside a container.
                    

                    

                        
                        

                            Clearing the bot check on a cold profile has been measured at over a minute; once the
                            stored session holds the clearance a render takes a few seconds.
                        

                    

                    

                        
                        

                            A Chrome, Chromium or Edge binary. ThemeForge prefers its own downloaded copy, then
                            whatever is installed on the server.
                        

                    

                    

                        
                        

                            Used for the plain request and given to the browser — Chrome's own headless user
                            agent says “HeadlessChrome”, which IMDb refuses outright. A user agent going
                            stale is the usual reason this stops working, so moving it on is the first thing to try.

Removing themes

                    

                        Deletes every theme.mp3 ThemeForge wrote into your library. Files you have
                        replaced by hand are left alone — each one is checked against the contents
                        ThemeForge recorded when it wrote it, and anything that no longer matches is kept.
                        


                        Uninstalling ThemeForge does not do this. It removes the plugin's own data and
                        leaves your theme files where they are.
                    

                    

                    
Starting over

                    

                        Throws away every decision ThemeForge has recorded, so your library is looked at again
                        from nothing. Worth doing after an upgrade that changes how matches are chosen: a
                        recorded decision is only as good as the code that made it, and several of the states
                        they are kept in — rejected, locked — are treated as your judgement and
                        would never be revisited on their own.
                        


                        Ticking the box also deletes the theme files ThemeForge wrote. Anything you have
                        replaced by hand since is kept either way.
